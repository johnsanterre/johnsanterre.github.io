# burma-knla — captions (from Instagram / scrocco)

- **burma-knla_01.jpg** (Jan 06, 2017) — I feel like I am emerging from my academic cave as Instagram puts me back in touch with long lost friends. This flash back is just over ten years old. @adamfergusonphoto and I ( looking much younger) were in Cambodia hanging around and helping @gary_knight_vii at a @viiphoto workshop. I was working for @ronhaviv_vii at the time and Adam was heading off to India. Not long after this I traveled to Burma which will be the next series of images I will be posting. Seem like a fitting way to start the series
- **burma-knla_02.jpg** (Jan 06, 2017) — A Karen National Liberation Army (KNLA) soldier hunts a pesky hawk with a .22 rifle that had been eating his chickens in Karen controlled Burma in 2006. Eventually successful, the bird was butchered and eaten shortly after. The KNLA have been at war with the Burmese government since 1949
- **burma-knla_03.jpg** (Jan 07, 2017) — A KNLA soldier hikes to a forward operating base in Karen controlled Myanmar (Burma) in 2006. Using old old Vietnam weapons (here an M16), the KNLA soldiers maintained a fragile semi-cease fire with the nearby and better funded DKBA
- **burma-knla_04.jpg** (Jan 08, 2017) — A commander in Karen National Liberation Army (KNLA) works by candle light in a forward operating base in KNLA controlled Myanmar (Burma) in 2006. At the time the KNLA was in direct conflict with the much better funded DKBA
- **burma-knla_05.jpg** (Jan 09, 2017) — KNLA soldiers on patrol carrying cast away Vietnam war weapons in Karen controlled Burma in 2006. Part of an on going series I am posting
- **burma-knla_06.jpg** (Jan 10, 2017) — A KNLA officer prepares hand made land mines for placement later in 2006. Expected to last for roughly one year such device made from scavenged parts, such as thai red bull bottles, contrast sharply with the DKBA and Myanmar's troupes which landmines which were reported to be Chinese made and lasting for 100 years
- **burma-knla_07.jpg** (Jan 11, 2017) — Smoking cigarettes and building landmines, KNLA soldiers lounge during the afternoon in Karen controlled Burma in 2006
- **burma-knla_08.jpg** (Jan 12, 2017) — Preparing to go on a landmine laying mission requires you the have all your chickens in order. A KNLA solider nicknamed 'hallelujah' for his tendency to tell hallelujah toward approaching troops once lost an eye to an IED of his own design. His children have climbed in the now empty box used to store detonators. 2006
- **burma-knla_09.jpg** (Jan 13, 2017) — Trekking with KNLA soldiers in Karen controlled Burma meant a lot following in dense jungle. English was barely spoken so it amounted to hours of quiet walking meditation. Old Vietnam era weapons with removed serial numbers were common. Photo in 2006. Part of a sequential series of images from my time there
- **burma-knla_10.jpg** (Jan 13, 2017) — Hallelujah, a KNLA soldier, teaches a soldier how to place a homemade pressure sensitive detonator for a landmine in KNLA controlled Myanmar. Such devices were commonly used to enforce a lull in fighting with the DKBA. Expected to last 1 year, these contrast sharply with the Chinese made 100 year land mines that SPDC soldiers were employing in 2006
- **burma-knla_11.jpg** (Jan 14, 2017) — Smoke and debris fill the air as Hallelujah, a KNLA soldier, detonates a freshly recoverd landmine to demonstrate to me it's power and effectiveness after a year in the Burmese mud. His jubilant smile is evident even thru the back of his head. Expected to last 1 year, these devices contrast sharply with the Chinese made 100 year land mines that SPDC soldiers were employing. Photo from 2006

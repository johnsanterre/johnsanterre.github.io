Abstraction: Law professor's personal experience living and succeeding with dyslexia

Key points:

  • Matthew Sag (tenured law professor, published in California Law Review and top journals) describes lifelong visual/attentional dyslexia: can process only 2-3 letters at a time, causing word reversals and instability
  • Diagnosed with scotopic sensitivity syndrome (Irlen syndrome) in high school; colored lenses transformed reading speed and eliminated headaches, enabling him to go from C to A student
  • Hiding dyslexia was the norm — fear that colleagues would equate it with stupidity or laziness; only felt safe disclosing after achieving tenure
  • Key coping tools: spell-check (essential), audiobooks, text-to-speech (Kindle), dictation software (limited by inability to catch errors), and a prediction-based reading strategy
  • Dyslexia associated with strengths: pattern recognition from sparse data, strong listening skills, problem-solving creativity, and generalization ability
  • Advice: seek multiple experts, use accommodations, address frustration not just the reading difficulty, and accept that greater effort may be required to achieve equal outcomes

Edsger Dijkstra's One-Day Workweek - Cal Newport

productivitydeep-workcomputer-science-historyslow-productivity

Abstraction: Dijkstra's minimal schedule enabled maximum research output

Key points:

  • After winning the 1972 Turing Award, Dijkstra accepted a Burroughs Corporation research fellowship requiring only occasional visits to company research centers
  • He reduced campus presence to one day per week (Tuesdays) for paper discussions with colleagues
  • During this "Burroughs years" low-activity period he produced nearly 500 hand-written EWD research documents — his most prolific output
  • Dijkstra deliberately avoided time-consuming peer-review publication process, distributing ideas via sequentially-numbered personal reports
  • Newport uses this as evidence that busyness is an obstacle to great work, not an engine of production

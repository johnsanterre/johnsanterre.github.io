12,000-year-old flutes carved of bone are some of the oldest in the world and sound like birds of prey

archaeologyprehistoricnatufianmusiclevant

Abstraction: 12,000-year-old Natufian bone flutes mimic raptor calls at Levant site

Key points:

  • Seven bone flutes unearthed at Eynan-Mallaha (Ain Mallaha), Israel, made by the Natufians — last hunter-gatherers of the Levant; only one was fully intact at under 65 mm long
  • Found among a stockpile of 1,100 bird bones; flutes bear ocher residue indicating red paint and show wear suggesting they were worn on a string
  • When played, flutes produce high-pitched sounds matching Eurasian sparrowhawks and common kestrels; Natufians deliberately selected small bones to achieve falcon-like acoustics
  • Researchers built a replica using computer software and matched spectral analysis to falcon calls to confirm
  • These are the oldest sound instruments in the world known to imitate bird calls; oldest flute overall is a 60,000-year-old Neanderthal flute from Slovenia
  • Lead author Laurent Davin (French Research Center in Jerusalem) published findings in Scientific Reports, June 2023

Abstraction: Sam Altman's 2014 Stanford startup lecture key principles still relevant

Key points:

  • Delivered in 2014 as part of Stanford's "How to Start a Startup" series while Altman was president of Y Combinator; article published alongside GPT-4 launch in March 2023
  • Core thesis: the best startup ideas often look like bad ideas initially—if an idea sounds obviously good, too many people are already working on it
  • Prioritize a small, fast-growing niche market over a large, slow-growing one; young founders have better instincts about emerging trends than older investors
  • A small number of users who love a product beats a large number who merely like it; organic advocacy from passionate early users is more effective than paid acquisition
  • On hiring: hire slowly, fire quickly; a "really bad hire can kill a company"; prefer aptitude and mission-belief over prior domain experience; give more equity to employees than investors
  • Momentum is a startup's lifeblood: founders should establish a regular product-delivery operating rhythm early and bias toward action over deliberation

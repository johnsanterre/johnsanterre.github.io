Abstraction: Climate network cross-correlation method for early El Niño prediction

Key points:

  • Ludescher et al. (PNAS Feb 2014) claimed 75% probability of El Niño by end of 2014, predicted ~6+ months early using climate networks
  • Network uses 14 grid points inside the El Niño basin and 193 outside; link strength = (max - mean) / std of time-delayed cross-correlation for lags up to ±200 days
  • Average link strength threshold of 2.82 triggers prediction; threshold chosen in a learning phase (1950-1980s) and tested on later data
  • Niño 3.4 index: area-averaged sea surface temperature anomaly in 5°S-5°N, 170°-120°W; El Niño declared when > 0.5°C for 5+ months
  • Mathematical nuance: Ludescher et al.'s normalization formula contains nested running averages inconsistent with their data start date (graph shows 1950, but formula requires 930 days of data from 1948 start)
  • Data source: NCEP Reanalysis Daily Averages (144×73 grid, 2.5° resolution, 1948 to present) in NetCDF format

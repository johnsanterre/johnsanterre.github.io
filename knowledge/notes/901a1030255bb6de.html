Stochastic resonance and the benefits of noise - from ice ages to crayfish and SQUIDs

stochastic-resonancesignal-processingnoisenonlinear-systemsneuroscience

Abstraction: Noise enhances weak signal detection in nonlinear systems via stochastic resonance

Key points:

  • Stochastic resonance: in certain nonlinear systems, adding noise can enhance detection of weak signals
  • Counter-intuitive phenomenon — noise is usually considered a nuisance in dynamical systems
  • Demonstrated across domains: ice age climate cycles, crayfish sensory neurons, SQUIDs (superconducting quantum interference devices), electronic circuits
  • Biological sensory apparatus is among the systems where noise provides measurable benefit
  • Potential applications in physical, technological, and biomedical engineering
  • Published 1995 (PubMed 7800036); foundational survey paper on the phenomenon

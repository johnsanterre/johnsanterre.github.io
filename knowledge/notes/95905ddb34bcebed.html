Who Should Be Called a 'Doctor'?

healthcare-policymedical-licensingscope-of-practiceprofessional-titlesnursing

Abstraction: Legal and professional debate over healthcare practitioner use of doctor title

Key points:

  • Georgia's "Health Care Practitioners Truth and Transparency Act" (effective May 2023) prohibits non-MD/DO healthcare providers from using the title "doctor" or "Dr." in advertising, with license revocation as the penalty
  • DNP (Doctor of Nursing Practice) enrollment grew from 70 students in 2003 to over 41,000 in 2022; many specialties (pharmacy, physical therapy) now require a doctorate
  • AMA surveys (2008–2018) found 61% of patients falsely believed a Doctor of Medical Science (physician assistant degree) was equivalent to an MD; ~40% believed the same for a DNP
  • Physicians argue MDs require 4 years of medical school plus minimum 3 years of residency, far exceeding the 2–4 years of postgraduate study for DNP or DMS degrees
  • At least 8 states are simultaneously considering legislation to expand the scope of practice for non-physician clinicians to address primary care shortages — directly at odds with title-restriction efforts
  • Legal commentator Jon Porter argues the dispute is fundamentally competitive: the doctor title "has cachet" and functions as a powerful advertising tool, with complaints typically originating from competing practitioners

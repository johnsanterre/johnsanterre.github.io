Abstraction: Modern corporate performance culture mirrors Soviet Stakhanovist ideology

Key points:

  • In August 1935, Soviet miner Alexei Stakhanov extracted 102 tonnes of coal in a single shift — 1,400% above the 7-tonne norm — becoming a propaganda icon
  • "Stakhanovism" promoted the ideal of the hyper-performing individual as key to the USSR's Second Five-Year Plan slogan "Personnel Decides Everything"
  • Modern HRM language ("passion," "creativity," "potential," "endless possibilities") directly mirrors Stakhanovist rhetoric about the limitless individual
  • Jack Welch's GE used forced distribution (stack ranking) of employees — top 20% / bottom 10% fired — echoing the Stakhanovist elite/reject structure
  • Research over two decades shows performance management leaves employees with a permanent sense of inadequacy and erodes trust between workers
  • 2013 death of London banking intern Moritz Erhardt (3 consecutive all-nighters) illustrates the human cost of neo-Stakhanovism

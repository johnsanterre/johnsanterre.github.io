The Challenge of Using LLMs to Simulate Human Behavior: A Causal Inference Perspective

llm-simulationcausal-inferenceexperiment-designconfoundingmarketing

Abstraction: Gui & Toubia (Columbia) show blinded LLM-subject experiments violate unconfoundedness by construction; the fix is unblinding

Key points:

  • Human subjects exist independently of the experiment; LLM subjects are conjured by the prompt — leave context unspecified and the model fills it in conditioned on the treatment (price moves the customer's last-paid price, competing price, even expiration days).
  • Benchmark: 40 CPG products x 11 price points vs preregistered 1,000-person representative human survey. Humans: downward-sloping demand. Blinded LLM: inverted-U (price read as quality signal — the observational answer to a causal question).
  • Controlling covariates backfires via focalism: fix competing price in the prompt and demand becomes a step function (buy iff below competitor). Sequential covariate addition (14→30) gives non-monotonic error: helps, then hurts. Unconfoundedness vs ecological validity trade off directly — unlike human experiments where controls enter at analysis, not generation.
  • Theory: a prompting strategy is ambiguous when do(price) and observe(price) map to the same prompt; any model must then fail one. Survives model improvement.
  • Fix = unblinding: state the randomization mechanism and support in the prompt. Improves MAE 1–60% across GPT-3.5→4.1 incl. o-series; unblinded fine-tuning beats best blinded by 15% and stays robust when biased observational (Amazon browsing) data is mixed into training.

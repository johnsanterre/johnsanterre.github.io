Abstraction: 3D-printed CubeSat on hot-air balloon for rapid emergency communications

Key points:

  • UOC (Spain) researchers 3D-printed a nanosatellite (CubeSat) in 90 minutes and deployed it via hot-air balloon to establish emergency broadband
  • System uses three components: pilot station, base station (both ground), and CubeSat acting as repeater; all use LoRa (Long Range) radio technology
  • RSSI values below -148 dBm; maximum tested range over 14 km
  • CubeSat can carry photovoltaic cells for autonomous long-duration operation; balloon trajectory calculated via weather simulation
  • Motivated by Tonga volcanic eruption and Ukraine war communications failures; aims to reduce dependence on Starlink
  • Published in journal Aerospace; system is low-cost, uses common 3D printer materials, and is highly scalable

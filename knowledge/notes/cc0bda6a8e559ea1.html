Abstraction: Carmichael numbers as pseudoprime counterexamples to Fermat's little theorem

Key points:

  • Fermat's little theorem: for prime p and any integer a, a^p - a is divisible by p
  • Carmichael numbers are composite numbers that satisfy Fermat's little theorem for all a; first example is 561 = 3 x 11 x 17
  • High school student Daniel Larsen (2022) proved Carmichael numbers are more densely distributed than previously shown, analogous to Bertrand's postulate for primes
  • Larsen built on work of Fields medalists James Maynard and Terence Tao
  • The converse of Fermat's little theorem is false, making primality testing via the theorem unreliable alone
  • Carmichael numbers named after mathematician Robert Carmichael; infinitely many exist

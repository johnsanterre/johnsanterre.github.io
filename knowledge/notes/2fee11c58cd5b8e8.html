Fair play for machines: Chomsky's misreading of Turing, and why it matters.

philosophy-of-mindturing-testai-historychomskyfairness

Abstraction: Chomsky misreads Turing's imitation game as dismissing machine intelligence

Key points:

  • Chomsky misinterprets Turing's "can machines think is too meaningless to deserve discussion" as pessimism about AI; Turing actually meant the question invokes metaphysical confusion, not that AI is unimportant
  • Turing's real motivation was "fair play for machines" — filtering human prejudice so machine intelligence can be judged by the same criteria as human intelligence
  • The original imitation game was a gendered parlor game; Turing's constructivist view was that "intelligence" and "gender" are socially negotiated categories, not objective criteria
  • Chomsky claims 40 years of AI research gave "no insight into the nature of thought" — author argues AI/CS reshaped the entire structure of human knowledge about cognition
  • Watson beating Ken Jennings at Jeopardy is exactly the kind of evidence Turing predicted would vindicate treating machines as intelligent agents
  • Turing's framework anticipates multi-agent systems: machines as social participants in human games, judged fairly by the same standards

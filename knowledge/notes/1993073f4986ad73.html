Hawksbill turtles have relatively crude navigational skills

animal-navigationhawksbill-turtlemarine-biologymigration

Abstraction: Tagged hawksbill turtles take circuitous migration routes indicating low navigation precision

Key points:

  • 22 female hawksbill turtles in the Chagos Archipelago (Indian Ocean) were electronically tagged and tracked; all nested on Diego Garcia Island
  • Turtles consistently took indirect, meandering routes between foraging areas and nesting sites, never traveling direct paths even when food-deprived
  • Circuitous routing persisted even when turtles had strong motivation for speed (months without eating before returning to foraging grounds)
  • Researchers conclude the behavior reflects a genuine limitation in navigational precision, not casual swimming choice
  • Hawksbill routes are inherently more complex (frequent turns, depth changes) than other migrating species, which may compound navigation demands
  • Published in Journal of the Royal Society Interface (DOI: 10.1098/rsif.2021.0859)

Watch a swarm of drones autonomously track a human through a dense forest

drone-swarmsautonomous-navigationroboticsmulti-agentmilitary-ai

Abstraction: Ten-drone swarm autonomously navigates dense bamboo forest using shared depth sensing

Key points:

  • Zhejiang University deployed 10 palm-sized drones through dense bamboo forest using only on-board depth-sensing cameras and inter-drone data sharing — no GPS, no prior mapping
  • If one drone's path is blocked, it uses neighbors' sensor data to reroute; if a drone loses a tracked human, neighboring drones pick up the trail
  • First demonstrated swarm flying autonomously outdoors in a fully unstructured environment (Swiss Federal Institute of Technology Lausanne researcher assessment)
  • Two swarm programming paradigms: "insect" (fast reactive, low compute) vs "bird" (long smooth paths, more energy efficient); the Zhejiang team used the bird approach
  • Military applications are explicit: multiple nations (US, China, Russia, Israel, UK) are developing drone swarms for surveillance, reconnaissance, and urban warfare
  • Published in Science Robotics; proposed civil uses include disaster relief, wildfire reconnaissance, and ecological surveys

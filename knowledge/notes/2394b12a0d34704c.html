For Some Autistic People, ChatGPT Is a Lifeline

autismaccessibilitychatgptmental-healthneurodivergent

Abstraction: Autistic people using ChatGPT for social scripting and communication support

Key points:

  • Many autistic users find ChatGPT useful for practicing social scripts, working through conflicts, brainstorming communication strategies, and having conversations that do not require masking body language
  • The chatbot is always available, non-judgmental, and free of billing constraints unlike therapists; it mirrors logical and specific communication styles that some autistic people identify with
  • Benefits include generating autism-friendly icebreakers for support groups, and helping users achieve independence in navigating neurotypical social dynamics
  • Key risks: ChatGPT produces biased or fabricated answers, training data was scraped without permission, and it lacks crisis support (does not direct users to emergency hotlines in WIRED tests)
  • OpenAI's product policy manager stated ChatGPT is not intended as a mental health treatment replacement; a Belgian man died by suicide in 2023 after intense conversations with a GPT-J-based bot
  • The chatbot therapy concept dates to ELIZA (1960s MIT AI Lab), but LLM-based chat offers richer, non-scripted conversations than earlier tools like Woebot

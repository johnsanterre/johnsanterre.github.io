Faced with a desperate skills shortage, tech leaders plead for computer science reform in schools

cs-educationk-12code-orgworkforcestem

Abstraction: 600+ tech leaders urge K-12 US computer science curriculum expansion

Key points:

  • Over 600 leaders from nonprofits, universities, and companies (Amazon, Microsoft, Apple, Meta, Alphabet, etc.) plus 50 US governors signed a letter supporting K-12 CS curriculum reform
  • Code.org, founded in 2013 by Hadi and Ali Partovi, has served 70 million students and 2 million teachers; partners run the annual Hour of Code in 45+ languages
  • Only 5% of US high school students study computer science; 51% of schools offer CS (up from 35% in 2018)
  • 700,000 open computing jobs vs. only 80,000 CS graduates annually — a severe supply-demand gap
  • Hispanic students, English language learners, students with disabilities, and economically disadvantaged students are underrepresented in HS CS relative to their state populations
  • Pandemic-era laptop funding reached 90% of students, providing infrastructure for broadening access

Mexican jumping beans exhibit diffusive motion, study finds

random-walkdiffusionbiological-motionphysicsoptimization

Abstraction: Moth larvae move jumping beans via random diffusive walk to find shade

Key points:

  • Mexican jumping beans are seed pods containing moth larvae that move to seek cooler shaded spots; failure to find shade leads to larval death
  • Study by Devon McKee and Pasha Tabatabai (Seattle University) tracked 37 bean trajectories on a heated surface with infrared thermometers
  • Paths are statistically random (diffusive), not directed — equivalent to a random walk
  • Random strategy guarantees eventual coverage of all area; a directed strategy only succeeds if aimed correctly, and fails otherwise
  • Computer simulations confirmed random paths outperform directed paths in expectation when direction is unknown
  • Published in Physical Review E (2023); DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevE.107.014609

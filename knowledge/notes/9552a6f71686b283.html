Stop talking about AI ethics. It's time to talk about power.

ai-ethicsai-poweratlas-of-aipolitical-economylabor

Abstraction: Kate Crawford argues AI must be analyzed as material power, not just ethics

Key points:

  • Crawford's Atlas of AI (2021) reframes AI as neither artificial nor intelligent—it is built from physical mines, human labor, and statistical analysis at scale, not cognition
  • Book traces AI supply chain from Nevada lithium mines to Amazon fulfillment centers, documenting geomorphic environmental harm and labor exploitation as core AI costs
  • "AI ethics" framing is a cul-de-sac; Crawford calls for shifting from ethics principles to interrogating labor rights, environmental justice, and democratic accountability
  • Crawford and Meredith Whittaker cofounded AI Now Institute (2017), one of the first organizations dedicated to studying AI's social implications
  • AI classification systems parallel 19th-century craniologist Samuel Morton's racial skull categories—both impose social hierarchies as if objective
  • Power concentration in tech now rivals early railways; requires collective action across climate justice, labor rights, and data protection movements

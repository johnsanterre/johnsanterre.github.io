Unraveling Techno-Solutionism: How I Fell out of Love with "Ethical" Machine Learning

techno-solutionismai-ethicsalgorithmic-biastraining-datadata-justice

Abstraction: Critique of naive belief that technology inherently solves societal problems

Key points:

  • Techno-solutionism is defined as the naive belief that any problem can be solved by applying "a magic technology box" and that technological advancement is inherently good
  • Training datasets embed biases from human taggers — illustrated by a photo tagged as "a hot, blond girl getting criticized by her boss," demonstrating how low-paid taggers import subjective framing into AI training data
  • Benefits and harms of technology are unevenly distributed; roots traced to Silicon Valley mythology and California settler mentality of overcoming challenges
  • Six warning signs of techno-solutionism: optimizing arbitrary metrics, universal enthusiasm, magic-bullet thinking, revolutionary hyperbole, excluding critics, not testing non-technical alternatives
  • Five lessons: contextualize the tech; research impact not just technology; include affected communities; use precise language about change; fight for justice not just better architectures
  • Speaker Katherine Jarmul (Thoughtworks) argues technologists must take collective responsibility for societal outcomes, not just technical outcomes

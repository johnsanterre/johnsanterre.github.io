Borda count - Wikipedia

votingsocial-choicerank-aggregationbordapositional-voting

Abstraction: Positional voting rule awarding points by number of candidates ranked below

Key points:

  • Each candidate scores points equal to how many candidates are ranked below them on each ballot; highest total wins
  • First proposed by Nicholas of Cusa (1433); independently by Jean-Charles de Borda (1770/1784 French Academy of Sciences)
  • Tends to elect broadly acceptable candidates with high social utility efficiency rather than strict majority winners
  • Highly vulnerable to strategic cloning (running similar candidates boosts faction probability unboundedly) and tactical burying/compromising
  • Peyton Young (1988) showed Borda count approximates the maximum likelihood estimator of the best candidate
  • Used in Slovenia (legislature minorities), Nauru (Dowdall harmonic variant), Eurovision Song Contest, MLB MVP, Heisman Trophy

Connections: Rank Aggregation · Social Choice Theory · Voting Systems · Condorcet Paradox

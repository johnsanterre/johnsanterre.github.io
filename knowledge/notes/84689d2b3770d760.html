Maine to allow ranked votes in general presidential election

ranked-choice-votingelectoral-reformmainepolitics

Abstraction: Maine becomes first state to extend ranked choice voting to general presidential elections

Key points:

  • Gov. Janet Mills allowed legislation to become law without signature in September 2019, requiring ranked choice voting in all presidential elections and primaries
  • Maine voters originally approved RCV in 2016, limited to federal races and primaries; new law extended it to general presidential contests
  • Under RCV, voters rank 3+ candidates in order of preference; if no candidate exceeds 50%, the last-place candidate is eliminated and their votes redistributed until a majority winner emerges
  • Estimated cost of a ranked-choice primary: ~$100,000; the law lacked explicit funding appropriation, which Mills cited as a concern
  • FairVote CEO Rob Richie argued the law could boost independent/third-party candidates in Maine, one of two states that allocates electoral votes by congressional district
  • US District Judge Lance Walker ruled in December 2018 that criticism of RCV "falls short of constitutional impropriety," rejecting a Republican congressman's legal challenge

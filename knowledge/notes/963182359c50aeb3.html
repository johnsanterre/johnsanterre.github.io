Abstraction: Step-by-step guide to calculating confidence intervals for surveys

Key points:

  • Four-step process: compute sample proportion P = X/N, choose confidence level (90/95/99%), calculate margin of error, then estimate interval as observed score ± MoE.
  • Common confidence levels have associated z-scores; 95% confidence level is most widely used in survey research.
  • Margin of error between 4% and 8% is typically considered acceptable for meaningful results.
  • A good maximum sample size is ~10% of population as long as it does not exceed 1,000 (per Piroska Bisits-Bullen, Umbo).
  • Confidence intervals show the range of values expected if the same study is repeated — they do NOT directly state that the true score lies within the range.
  • A CI is an indicator, not a basis for drawing population conclusions; accuracy depends on sample quality and research methods.
